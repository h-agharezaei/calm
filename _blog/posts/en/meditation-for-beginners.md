Many people think meditation means sitting perfectly still for hours and thinking about nothing. Getting started is actually much simpler: a few minutes of sitting and practicing bringing your attention back to one thing, such as your breath or a sound.

## What is meditation?

Meditation is a broad name for a family of attention practices. In its most common form, you rest your attention on something simple, like your breath, and each time your mind wanders off, you bring it back. The goal isn't an empty mind; it's *noticing* and *returning*.

Some common forms of meditation:

- **Breath awareness:** following the sensation of air moving in and out.
- **Sound meditation:** using a steady sound, like waves or a river, as your anchor.
- **Observing thoughts:** watching thoughts come and go like clouds, without following them.
- **Body scan:** slowly moving your attention through the body, from head to toe.

## Your first 5-minute meditation

1. **Place:** sit somewhere quiet. A chair is perfectly fine; you don't need to sit cross-legged.
2. **Body:** back straight but not stiff, shoulders relaxed, hands resting on your legs.
3. **Eyes:** close them, or let your gaze rest softly on a spot on the floor.
4. **Breath:** take a few normal breaths and notice where you feel the air most: nose, chest or belly.
5. **Return:** every time your mind drifts to a thought, silently say "thinking" and come back to the breath.
6. **Finish:** when the bell rings, wait a moment, then slowly open your eyes.

## What to do with a busy mind

A busy mind means meditation is working, not that you've failed. You might get distracted twenty times in five minutes, which means you've practiced coming back twenty times. Be kind to yourself and don't chase a "perfect" session.

If starting with the breath feels hard, start with sound. Ocean or river sounds are a bigger, easier anchor; that's exactly what the [10-minute ocean sounds meditation](/en/blog/meditation-10-sea-ocean/) is for. If you feel restless, a few rounds of [box breathing](/en/blog/box-breathing/) before you sit can help you settle into a slower rhythm.

## How to make meditation a habit

- **Start small.** Five minutes every day beats an hour once a week.
- **Attach it to something you already do.** For example, after brushing your teeth in the morning or before bed.
- **Pick a regular spot.** A familiar corner of your home helps your mind get ready.
- **Keep your tools simple.** A timer with a start and end bell is enough, so you don't worry about the clock.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I meditate?
Five to ten minutes is enough to start. Once it feels comfortable, you can gradually make it longer.

### Is it better to meditate in the morning or at night?
The best time is the one you can stick to every day. Some people prefer the morning; others like the evening before bed.

### Is meditation different from yoga?
Yoga focuses mostly on movement and body postures combined with breathing, while meditation is usually done sitting still with a focus on attention. The two are often practiced together.

### Do I need to install an app to meditate?
No. Open Calm right in your browser; it's free and needs no sign-up. You can also add it to your phone's home screen if you like.
