To do 4-7-8 breathing, inhale quietly through your nose for a count of 4, hold your breath for 7, then exhale slowly through your mouth for 8. Start with four rounds, which takes a little over a minute. This guide covers the steps, the mistakes that make it harder than it needs to be, who should be careful, and what the research does and doesn't show, including the popular claim that it helps you fall asleep.

## What is 4-7-8 breathing?

The 4-7-8 technique was popularized by the American physician Andrew Weil, who calls it the "relaxing breath", and is usually described as rooted in yogic breath control (pranayama). Its defining features are an exhale twice as long as the inhale and a long hold in between.

One round takes 19 seconds, so you breathe roughly three times a minute. That's far slower than normal breathing at rest, which puts 4-7-8 in the broader family of slow-breathing exercises.

The numbers describe a ratio rather than exact seconds. If you count faster or slower, that's fine; what matters is keeping the 4 : 7 : 8 proportion.

## Does 4-7-8 breathing work? What the research says

You'll often read that 4-7-8 breathing will "put you to sleep in 60 seconds". That's a popular claim, not a research finding. Studies of this specific technique are few and small:

- In a study of 43 healthy young adults, researchers saw changes in heart rate variability and blood pressure during 4-7-8 breathing, which they interpreted as favorable, especially in participants who weren't sleep-deprived ([Vierra et al., *Physiological Reports*, 2022](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.15389)). The study did not measure sleep quality.
- In a trial with 90 patients recovering from bariatric surgery, the group that practiced 4-7-8 breathing reported lower state anxiety after six hours than the deep-breathing and usual-care groups ([Aktaş & İlgin, *Obesity Surgery*, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36480101/)). That's a specific hospital setting, and it doesn't generalize to everyone.

There's more evidence for slow breathing in general. A systematic review of 15 studies found that breathing at fewer than 10 breaths per minute was linked to measurable changes in heart rhythm and brain activity, and to reports of greater relaxation, while noting that the mechanisms are still unclear ([Zaccaro et al., *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00353)). A meta-analysis of 12 randomized trials found a small-to-medium effect of breathwork on self-reported stress, but the samples were small and most studies had a moderate risk of bias ([Fincham et al., *Scientific Reports*, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-27247-y)).

The honest summary: 4-7-8 breathing is a simple, free way to pause and slow your breathing. Nobody has shown that it works for everyone, or that it's better for sleep than other techniques.

## How to do 4-7-8 breathing, step by step

1. **Sit comfortably.** Back upright but not stiff, shoulders relaxed. You can do it in bed, but sitting is easier the first few times.
2. **Breathe out fully.** Let all the air out once, gently, through your mouth.
3. **Inhale (count of 4):** close your mouth and breathe in quietly through your nose.
4. **Hold (count of 7):** hold the breath without straining.
5. **Exhale (count of 8):** breathe out slowly through your mouth. In the common version the tip of your tongue rests behind your upper front teeth and the exhale makes a soft "whoosh", but that part is optional.
6. **Repeat.** That's one round. Do four, then go back to normal breathing for a few breaths.

### How many rounds should a beginner do?

Most guides suggest four rounds to start, building up to no more than eight after a few weeks of practice. The reason is simple: long holds and long exhales can make you lightheaded if you're not used to them. More rounds is not automatically better.

## Common mistakes

- **Straining during the hold.** If seven feels hard, count faster or use a shorter ratio. The hold shouldn't involve clenching or locking your throat.
- **Counting too fast.** When you're in a hurry, a whole round can shrink to eight seconds. Keep the count slow and even.
- **Inhaling too deeply.** You don't need to fill your lungs to the limit. A calm, full breath is enough.
- **Too many rounds at first.** Ten rounds on day one is more likely to make you dizzy than calm.
- **Expecting instant results.** If you're not asleep or relaxed after four rounds, you haven't failed. The goal is a short pause, not a guaranteed state.

## Who should be careful

Slow breathing is low-risk for most people, but this technique includes breath holding:

- If you feel dizzy, lightheaded, short of breath or notice your heart racing, stop and breathe normally.
- If you have a heart condition, uncontrolled high blood pressure, asthma or another lung condition, or you're pregnant, talk to your doctor before practicing exercises with breath holds.
- If focusing on your breath makes you anxious, or you've had panic attacks, start with fewer rounds and stop if it makes things worse.
- Breathing exercises are not a treatment for insomnia, anxiety or any medical condition. If sleep problems or anxiety last for weeks, see a doctor or a qualified professional.

## 4-7-8 breathing vs box breathing

[Box breathing](/en/blog/box-breathing/) has four equal steps (inhale, hold, exhale, pause, four seconds each) and an even rhythm. 4-7-8 has three steps, an exhale twice as long as the inhale, and a longer cycle.

- **Box breathing** is easier to start with and easy to remember. Many people use it for a quick pause during the day.
- **4-7-8 breathing** has a longer hold and takes a bit of practice. Many people choose it for the evening, although no study has shown it's better than other techniques for that.

If the seven-second hold feels like too much, start with box breathing and come back to 4-7-8 later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does 4-7-8 breathing help you sleep?
Some people find it helps them wind down at bedtime, but there are no controlled studies showing that it improves sleep. It can be part of a calm evening routine, as long as you don't expect a miracle.

### How often can I do 4-7-8 breathing?
There's no fixed rule. Common guidance is twice a day, four rounds at a time. Start small and pay attention to how your body responds.

### What if I can't hold my breath for 7 seconds?
Count faster so the ratio stays the same, or start with a shorter version such as 2-3.5-4. In Calm you can also use the "Custom" pattern to set the length of each step yourself.

### Do I have to exhale through my mouth?
That's the common version, because a slow mouth exhale is easier to control. If it's uncomfortable, breathe out through your nose; what matters is that the exhale is slow.
