Most of our day runs on autopilot: we answer messages, work, drink coffee, and suddenly it's night without once asking "how am I actually doing right now?" This practice is a small way to interrupt that autopilot, and it takes about thirty seconds each time.

The idea is simple: every two hours a soft chime sounds, you step away from what you're doing for half a minute, and you do two things: ask yourself one question, and deliberately pick a state of mind.

## Where the idea comes from

The practice is inspired by the concept of a "state of being" that Joe Dispenza describes in his books and talks: the combination of thoughts, feelings and inner mood you carry at any moment. He argues that much of our day is spent in states carried over from the past, such as worry, doubt or replaying old events.

An honest caveat: "state of being" is a popular, experience-based idea, not an established scientific finding, and this article makes no medical or scientific claims. But the practice itself, pausing every few hours to notice how you are, is simple and stands on its own. You can try it without buying into any particular framework.

## The three-step practice

With each chime:

1. **Pause.** Stop what you're doing, drop your shoulders and take one slow breath.
2. **Ask:** "Am I just continuing yesterday's usual state, or am I choosing the one I want?" You don't need a polished answer. Just notice.
3. **Create one feeling on purpose for 30 seconds.** Curiosity, gratitude or hope, for example. Think of a situation that brings it up and let it stay in your body for half a minute.

Then go back to your work. That's all.

## Set a 2-hour chime in Calm

You don't need another alarm app. Calm has a periodic chime, and you only need to set its interval to two hours. The built-in presets run from 3 to 15 minutes, so use the "Custom" option:

1. Open [Calm](https://tpis.ir/?lang=en).
2. Tap the **Sound Interval Settings** icon.
3. Choose **Custom**.
4. In "Sound Playback Interval (seconds)" enter **7200** (2 hours × 60 minutes × 60 seconds).
5. Press **Update Interval**, then press "Start".
6. If the chime is too loud, lower the **chime volume**. A gentle sound is plenty for this practice.

The first chime plays right when you press Start, so you can treat it as chime number one and do the practice immediately. After that, it sounds every two hours.

## One limitation worth knowing

Calm's periodic chime works while the page is open and the device is awake. Browsers slow down or suspend background pages, and if a chime would be more than about 90 seconds late (for example after a laptop sleeps), Calm skips it rather than ringing out of nowhere.

So this works best for **a workday at a computer**: keep Calm open in a tab. On a phone, the chime may not ring once the screen locks. If your phone will be locked, a regular phone alarm is more reliable.

## Tips for sticking with it

- **Tie the first chime to a habit.** Open Calm when you turn on your computer.
- **Keep it short.** Thirty seconds is enough. If it gets heavy, it won't last.
- **Don't fake the feeling.** If you're tired or irritated at that moment, just notice it. Noticing without judging is part of the practice.
- **If you miss a few chimes,** you haven't failed. Pick it up again with the next one.
- **Write the question down** ("What state am I in?") on a sticky note near your screen so it's ready when the chime rings.

If you'd like your pauses to go a little deeper, try a short [box breathing](/en/blog/box-breathing/) round now and then. And if meditation is new to you, the [beginner's guide to meditation](/en/blog/meditation-for-beginners/) is a good next read.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why two hours? Can it be shorter or longer?
Two hours isn't magic. It's short enough to break the day into a few segments and long enough not to get in the way. If it feels too much or too little, enter any number of seconds in the settings, for example 3600 for every hour.

### Do I have to create a positive feeling?
No. The goal is to have a moment of choice instead of an automatic reaction. If creating a feeling seems forced, just ask the second question and watch your breath.

### Does this replace therapy or counseling?
No. This is an everyday awareness practice. If you're dealing with anxiety, depression or something serious, please talk to a professional.

### Will the chime ring if my phone locks?
It may not. Calm's chime depends on the page staying open, so it suits computer work better.
